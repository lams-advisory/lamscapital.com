Does NIS2 apply to your company?

Probably yes, if both of these are true. The exact answer depends on your activity codes, your group structure and the national law.

  • Size: at least 50 employees, or annual turnover above €10 million (medium-sized and larger companies).
  • Sector: you operate in a sector listed by the directive, for example food production and distribution, manufacturing of machinery, electrical equipment, electronics, vehicles or medical devices, chemicals, transport, postal and courier services, or waste management.
  • Some smaller companies are also covered, regardless of size, when a member state designates them as critical.
  • Through your clients: companies covered by NIS2 must manage the security of their supply chain. Even if the law does not cover you directly, expect security questionnaires, audits and contract clauses from your largest clients. Without answers, contracts are at risk.

What the law asks of you

The directive sets the minimum; each member state adds its own procedure and deadlines.

Register and assess

Register with the national cybersecurity authority, then assess your risk level and the maturity of your security measures within the deadlines the authority sets.

Manage risk

Put in place measures that fit your risk: policies, access control, backups and recovery, supplier security, encryption, staff training and business continuity.

Report incidents

Early warning within 24 hours of a significant incident, notification within 72 hours and a final report within one month.

Involve management

Management approves the measures, oversees them and follows cybersecurity training. Breaches can lead to fines and to measures against the people in charge.

In Romania

NIS2 is transposed by Government Emergency Ordinance 155/2024, approved by Law 124/2025, and enforced by the National Cybersecurity Directorate (DNSC). Companies must register within 30 days of falling under the law, and the DNSC began applying fines in September 2026. Read the Romanian page.
